overextracted

Criterion Coffee Club puts $40 million a year into five paid channels. Every dashboard says it is working. A Bayesian marketing mix model built on 40 markets and 104 weeks of geo data says that money causes about $16 million of revenue, and that 61 percent of the budget sits in the two channels least able to justify themselves. This is the case for moving it, and what it costs to leave it alone.

The call

Three moves, worth $4 million a year, and the one that gets made for you if you wait

Criterion's media plan is not too big. It is pointed at the wrong things. The line that matters to finance

Cutting the budget by a fifth, to $32 million, and reallocating what is left produces more incremental revenue than today's full $40 million. That is $8 million handed back to the P&L with revenue going up, not down.

The uncomfortable part is that a 20 percent cut is a plausible thing for a CFO to ask for anyway. If that request lands and there is no measurement point of view in the room, the cut gets applied across the board, because that is the only defensible way to cut without one. Same $32 million, $4.4 million a year worse. The decision is not whether to cut. It is whether the cut is aimed.

The brief

A brand that looks like it is winning

Criterion is a premium direct to consumer coffee subscription. Revenue is steady, the subscriber base is loyal and the media plan has grown into TikTok and connected TV over two years. Every platform dashboard reports a healthy return. Finance divides revenue by media spend and gets six to one.

None of those numbers answer the question leadership is actually asking, which is what happens to revenue if the money moves. A platform dashboard reports the conversions it saw. It cannot report the conversions that would have happened anyway, because it never observes them. That gap is not a reporting flaw, it is a structural one, and it is the reason a brand can grow for two years while its media plan quietly stops working.

The thesis

Three honest ways to read the same data. Three different answers.

Before any modeling, it is worth seeing how much the method decides the answer. Below are three readings of one dataset, none of them fraudulent, none of them agreeing.

The blended reading counts revenue that would have arrived anyway. The correlation reading goes negative for four of the five channels because Criterion pushes media hardest exactly when competitors do, and competitive pressure is the strongest single depressant of revenue in this data. A one channel regression hands that confounding to the channel. Medicine has a name for this shape of error, confounding by indication, from observing that sicker patients receive more aggressive treatment and concluding the treatment is killing them.

Why this decides budgets, not just charts

These three numbers do not disagree slightly. They differ by a factor of fifteen. Quote the first and every channel looks fundable and the plan never changes. Quote the second and you cancel media that works. The third is the only one that answers what happens if the money moves, which is the only question a planning cycle actually asks.

Causal design

What I decided before I touched the data

The model specification is where the analytical judgment lives, so it comes first and gets written down. Everything downstream is mechanics.

Why branded search is not in the model

Branded search sits downstream of everything else. Upper funnel video makes people go and type the brand name, so branded search volume is a consequence of the other channels rather than an independent cause. Controlling for a mediator would strip the video channels of the credit they are owed and hand it to search. It stays out, and that is a modeling decision I would defend rather than a data limitation.

The commercial version of that sentence: include branded search and the model will hand credit to the cheapest channel in the plan and take it from the ones building demand. Teams then defund the channels that feed the funnel, watch branded search decay two quarters later, and have no idea why.

The KPI

Weekly revenue per market, built as conversions multiplied by revenue per conversion, modeled per capita so that New York and Albuquerque contribute on comparable terms. Contributions are scaled back to dollars at the end.

Quality gate

The boring part that saves you later

A model cannot separate two things that always move together. This gate ran before estimation and had the authority to stop it.

This matters commercially because it decides whether a channel level recommendation is allowed at all. If two channels always flight together, the honest output is a recommendation about the pair, not about either one. Vendors who skip this step produce channel level precision they have no right to.

Estimation

Carryover, saturation and the parts I refused to let the optimizer choose

Media does not work in the week it runs and it does not work in a straight line. Both shapes have to be assumed before any return can be estimated, which is the uncomfortable centre of this whole method.

Adstock spreads each week of media over the following weeks with a geometric decay. A Hill curve then bends the accumulated media so that each additional impression is worth slightly less than the one before. I selected both on a 13 week out of sample window rather than in sample fit, and I bounded the search on purpose.

Left unbounded, the search wandered to a decay of 0.8 and a saturation slope steep enough to be a step function, and it bought about one tenth of one percent in holdout error for that. A step function says advertising does nothing at all and then everything at one exact spend level. No media team can plan against that, so I kept the grid inside ranges I could explain out loud and took the marginally worse fit.

What carryover is worth in a planning conversation

Adstock decay converts to a half life. Search comes back at about six days, Meta at about nine and a half, and YouTube, TikTok and CTV at none at all, meaning whatever they do lands in the week they run and is gone.

That is directly actionable. Channels with no memory should be flighted against demand, not run continuously for presence. Three of the five channels here are being run as if they build something that lasts, and in this data they do not.

Priors, and whether they mattered

Coefficients are constrained non negative and given half normal priors set in return terms, centered so the prior median return is about 1.5 with meaningful mass out to four. I ran the model a second time with priors twenty times looser to check whether the answer was mine or the data's.

Diagnostics

Did the sampler converge, and did prediction accuracy earn any trust

Four chains, 1000 warmup draws and 1500 kept draws each. Convergence is a statement about the arithmetic, not about whether the causal story is right, and those two get confused constantly.

Out of sample fit on the final 13 weeks lands at almost exactly the in sample number, so the model is not memorizing. That is reassuring about the baseline and says nothing at all about whether media contribution is right. A model can predict revenue well by leaning on trend and seasonality while getting every channel wrong. Prediction is a smoke alarm here, not the deliverable.

Worth knowing when a vendor presents to you: accuracy and causal validity are different claims, and accuracy is the easy one. If a deck leads with R squared and never shows what happens when the identifying variation is taken away, the second claim has not been tested.

Decomposition

What is actually holding up $247 million

For a subscription business this shape is not a scandal. Most revenue in any given week comes from people who already subscribed, and the job of media is to add to that base and slow the leak. Media should be judged against the part it moves, not against the total it sits next to.

How to say this to a CMO without it becoming a budget cut

An 88 percent baseline is normal for an established subscription brand and it is not evidence that marketing is failing. It is evidence that the marketing already worked, and that the base it built is now the largest asset the company has.

The risk in this number is not that someone cuts media. It is that someone cuts it indiscriminately. Baseline is not permanent, it decays when acquisition stops, and it decays on a lag long enough that nobody connects the two events. That is the argument for aiming the budget rather than shrinking it.

Saturation

The extraction window

Coffee people already have the right mental model for this. Pull a shot too short and you under extract, and the cup is thin and sour. Pull it too long and you over extract, and you are pushing water through spent grounds for bitterness. There is a window in between where the yield is worth the water.

Media spends the same way. Below the window each dollar buys reach you have not paid for yet. Inside it, the next dollar roughly pays for itself. Past it you are still spending and the cup is not getting better.

Medicine has the sharper version of this idea, the therapeutic window, where too small a dose does nothing and too large a one starts doing harm. Marketing has no toxicity term, which is precisely why budgets drift past the window and stay there. Nothing goes visibly wrong. The money simply stops working and the reporting does not have a way to say so.

Return is total incremental revenue divided by spend. Marginal return is what the next dollar buys, which is the number a budget decision should turn on. The final column is the share of posterior draws where that next dollar returns more than a dollar. Only Meta gets meaningfully off the floor.

The distinction that changes the meeting

Search returns 0.91x overall and 0.36x on the next dollar. Those are not in conflict. The first describes money already spent, the second describes money not yet committed, and only the second is a decision.

Almost every budget conversation is held using the first number. That single substitution is how a plan ends up 61 percent weighted toward channels whose next dollar does close to nothing, without anyone making an obviously bad call along the way.

A whole portfolio under breakeven is a warning about the level, not an instruction to stop

Every channel landing below a dollar of revenue per dollar spent is not a result to act on literally. Part of it is real for this business, because most weekly revenue renews whether or not media runs. Part of it is the model, which hands anything media cannot explain to the baseline and has no experiment underneath it to argue back.

So I would take the ordering and the curvature into a planning meeting and leave the absolute levels as a hypothesis. That is still enough to act on. You do not need to know TikTok's true return to know it is last, and last is where the money should come from.

Decision tool

Move the money yourself

This is the part a static deck cannot do. Every slider recomputes incremental revenue across 400 posterior draws, so the uncertainty travels with the recommendation instead of getting rounded away at the end.

Optimization is a greedy walk up the fitted response curves, spending each next increment wherever the marginal return is highest, with a floor of 20 percent of today's spend on every channel so nothing goes fully dark and leaves the model extrapolating. It also stops at 2.5 times today's spend, and Meta runs into that ceiling. The model has never seen Meta at that level, so the last part of that recommendation is extrapolation and should be treated as a test to run rather than a plan to book.

What I would tell the room, and what it costs to ignore it

Each of these is stated the way I would defend it: the signal, what I read into it, the action, and the consequence of leaving it alone. The last row is the one usually missing from measurement work, and it is the row that decides whether anything happens.

The cost of doing nothing

Inaction is not the neutral option. It is a decision to keep buying a specific outcome, and this is the price of it.

Three of those consequences are financial. The fourth is not, and it is the one I would raise last and most seriously. Every quarter this goes unmeasured, the argument gets harder to make, not easier. Budgets acquire constituencies. A line that has been $16 million for three years stops being a decision and becomes a baseline, defended by the people who built the plan around it. The cheapest moment to move this money is always the current one.

And the test itself decays. Geo holdouts read cleanest when a channel still has enough variation across markets to measure. TikTok is close to running everywhere, all the time, which is exactly the condition under which it becomes unmeasurable rather than merely unmeasured. Wait long enough and the option to find out goes away.

Sequencing, if I were running it

Move a third, measure, then decide the rest

Q1. Take TikTok to roughly $5M annualised. Move about a third of the freed budget into Search and Meta. Launch the geo holdout in matched markets on TikTok and CTV. Nothing here depends on the disputed part of the model.

Q2. Read the holdout. If measured lift agrees with the model, complete the reallocation and use the experiment to calibrate the priors. If it disagrees, the experiment wins and the model gets rebuilt around it. Either outcome is progress, which is the point of running it.

Q3 onward. Hold Meta below twice its current level until there is observed data up there. The optimiser wants 2.5x and has never seen it. That is the one recommendation on this page I would not book without evidence.

Where this breaks

The parts I would not bet the budget on

A case study that only shows the model working is a sales document. These are the three things that would make me slow down before acting on the numbers above.

And the data itself

This runs on Google's simulated Meridian panel dressed as a coffee brand, so cost per thousand impressions is perfectly constant within each channel and the markets are better behaved than any real client feed. Real data brings missing weeks, reclassified campaigns and a finance number that disagrees with the platform number. The method survives that. The tidiness of these particular results does not.

What I would do next

Run geo holdout tests on TikTok and CTV, which is where the model is most confident and most fragile at the same time. Two markets dark for six weeks would produce a directly measured lift that can calibrate the priors, and then the model stops being an argument and starts being an instrument. That is the project after this one.

Method note

How this was built

Mine

  • The estimand and the causal design, including the choice to exclude branded search as a mediator
  • Confounder selection and the decision to model per capita with market intercepts
  • Bounding the carryover and saturation search to ranges I can defend and rejecting the better fitting extreme
  • Prior setup in return terms and the sensitivity checks that test them
  • Every interpretation and recommendation on this page

AI assisted

  • Python scaffolding for the panel builder, the transforms and the Gibbs sampler
  • Convergence and effective sample size implementations, checked against known behavior
  • The charts and the front end on this page
  • Draft copy, edited

Estimation is a two stage approach rather than a single joint sampler. Carryover and saturation shapes are selected on a held out window, then coefficients are drawn with a conjugate Gibbs sampler with a non negativity constraint on all media. Google Meridian samples the shape parameters jointly, which propagates shape uncertainty into the intervals in a way this version does not. My credible bands are therefore narrower than they should be, and the sensitivity panel above is how I compensate for that in the meantime.

Data: geo_all_channels.csv from the Google Meridian repository, 40 markets by 156 weeks, last 104 weeks used. Spend and revenue are multiplied by one common factor so the case matches the $40 million brief. Ratios, shares and saturation shape are unchanged by that.
